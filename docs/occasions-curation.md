# Ysabel occasions: culinary research and display audit

Reviewed 10 September 2026. Planning range: 1 September 2026–1 January 2028.

## Repetition fix

Oktoberfest had one record per year, not duplicate database records. The calendar painted that record into every day between its start and end. Calendar cells now show starts only. Full date ranges remain on cards; an event crossing a month boundary appears once under “Continuing from last month”. Selecting a date still finds events running that day. Annual recurrences in different years remain intentional. Tests check every multi-day event, including Negroni Week and Songkran, for exactly one start marker.

## Selection approach

The expanded calendar has 143 dated records: the original 107 plus 36 occurrences of 26 newly researched annual occasions. The four editorial filters separate Asian, Italian, bar/wine and wider hospitality ideas. These are proposed brand fits, not confirmation that a particular dish, bottle or event is offered by Ysabel.

Prefer a named organiser, trade association, government cultural agency or UN body. When a useful day only has informal promotional standing, label it explicitly. Do not inflate the calendar with alternate national dates for the same product: for example, the Italian January pizza observance is selected instead of also adding the US February pizza day. Ramen Day and Noodle Day are distinct observances and have different suggested treatments.

## Strong culinary priorities — editorial recommendations

- Italian: Vera Pizza Day (17 January), Tiramisù Day (21 March), European Artisanal Gelato Day (24 March), Carbonara Day (6 April), World Aperitivo Day (26 May), Italian Republic Day (2 June), Ferragosto (15 August), World Pasta Day (25 October) and World Olive Tree Day (26 November).
- Asian: Lunar New Year and reunion dining, Hinamatsuri (3 March), Songkran (13–15 April), International Tea Day (21 May), Sushi Day (18 June, informal), Tanabata (7 July observance), Japanese Ramen Day (11 July), Dumpling Day (26 September, informal), Sake Day (1 October), Noodle Day (6 October, US informal), Kimchi Day (22 November) and Washoku Day (24 November).
- Bar: Riesling Birthday (13 March), Margarita Day (22 February, informal), Malbec World Day (17 April), Aperitivo Day, existing Cocktail/Gin/Whisky/Martini days, Prosecco Day (13 August, informal), Negroni Week and Champagne Day (fourth Friday of October).
- Across both kitchens: Pulses Day (10 February), Sustainable Gastronomy Day (18 June), Vegetarian Day (1 October), Chefs Day (20 October), Vegan Day (1 November), sourcing and food-waste occasions.

Use only a handful as major campaigns; the rest can support a dish portrait, chef interview, pairing, or community caption. Cultural and religious celebrations need appropriate context, not interchangeable “Asian” decoration. Alcohol-led ideas are for adult audiences and can include alcohol-free alternatives.

## Accuracy and uncertainty

Annual observance does not mean public holiday. Informal US product days are not presented as Asian national holidays. Sources are not live-synced. Review changing organiser dates before using them in public materials.

Italian Cuisine Week remains undated: the [Italian foreign ministry](https://www.esteri.it/it/temi/diplomazia_economica/promozione-integrata-del-sistema/settimana-della-cucina-italiana/) says that from 2025 diplomatic missions can schedule activities throughout the year, rather than only the traditional November week. Negroni Week 2027 and local festivals remain on the confirmation list. Existing tentative religious dates retain their labels. Existing bank holidays represent the celebration itself, not substitute closure dates.
